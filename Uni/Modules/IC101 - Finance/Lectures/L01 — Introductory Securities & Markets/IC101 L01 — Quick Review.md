# IC101 L01 — Quick Review
> [!abstract] 5-minute revision · Chapter 1
> **Finance allocates scarce resources across time and between people; it also assesses(оцениваеться), prices and transfers risk.**  
> Финансовая система помогает направлять capital туда, где он нужен, и распределять risk. **Price** передаёт рынку информацию, а **interest rate** — цена credit.

**Verified slides and practice:** [[IC101 L01 — Study Guide]] — слайд 27, Blackboard quiz, Seminar 1 и новые задачи.
**Exam focus:** 7 statements from the lecture slide. **Terms stay in English; explanations are simple Russian.**

## 1 · Finance, resources & price
- **Saving:** consume less than income today → resources available later.
- **Borrowing:** consume or invest today → repay from future income.
- **Intertemporal transfer:** shift *spending power* across time. **Intergenerational transfer:** resources shift across generations.
- **Financial investment:** buy shares/bonds; **Real investment:** buy machinery, factories, R&D or employ labour.
- **Price mechanism:** shortage → price tends to rise → production becomes more attractive → supply may expand. Financial markets use prices/yields to allocate capital.
- **Capital widening:** more capital to employ a growing workforce. **Capital deepening:** more capital *per worker*, potentially higher productivity.

> [!example] One chain
> Household savings → financial intermediaries / capital markets → company funding → real investment → potentially higher output and employment.

## 2 · Price vs value + Three Pillars
| Concept | Remember |
|---|---|
| **Market Price** | Observed trading price **now**. |
| **Fundamental Value / Intrinsic Value** | Estimate based on expected future cash flows and risk. |
| **Pillar 1: Time Value of Money** | Compare cash now with cash later. |
| **Pillar 2: Risk Management** | Identify, measure, reduce or transfer risks. |
| **Pillar 3: Asset Pricing** | Value assets using cash flows, timing and risk. |

**Market Price ≠ necessarily Fundamental Value.** A high quoted price does not prove an asset is valuable.

## 3 · Time Value of Money (TVM)
**Today’s money can be invested, so timing matters.**

$$FV=PV(1+r)^t$$
$$PV=\frac{FV}{(1+r)^t}$$

- $PV$: value today; $FV$: future amount; $r$: rate **per period**; $t$: number of periods.
- Example: **£1,100 in one year at 5%** → $PV=1100/1.05\approx \pounds1,047.62$.
- **Compounding:** move cash forward in time. **Discounting:** bring cash back to today.
- **NPV:** present value of future project inflows minus present value of outflows. **NPV > 0** → expected to add value *under the assumptions used*.
- **Opportunity Cost:** what you give up by choosing one option over another (e.g., foregone wages while studying).

## 4 · Risk & return
- **Risk:** outcome / return may differ from what was expected; finance often models this through variability.
- **Risk aversion:** most investors dislike risk for a given expected return; they demand compensation for extra risk.
- **Risk premium:** extra required expected return for bearing risk. **Higher risk does not guarantee higher realised return.**
- **Diversification:** spread exposures across assets (reduces concentration risk; *not* all market risk).
- **Hedging:** offset a particular exposure. **Insurance:** pay to transfer specified risks.
- Examples: **Credit Risk**, **Equity Risk**, **Interest Rate Risk**, **Currency Risk**, **Commodity Price Risk**.

## 5 · Asset pricing
- **DCF:** value = present value of risk-adjusted expected cash flows.
  $$V_0=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{E(CF_t)}{(1+r)^t}$$
- **Discount Rate:** often analysed as **risk-free rate + risk premium** (simplified).
- **Bonds:** when market yields **rise**, existing fixed-coupon bond prices generally **fall**; reverse when yields fall.
- **P/E = share price / earnings per share.** Compare similar firms; high P/E can reflect growth expectations *or* overvaluation.
- **P/B = market value of equity / book value of equity.** Often discussed for banks; P/B < 1 is a signal to investigate, not proof of accounting error.
- **Law of One Price:** identical payoffs and risks should have equal prices in frictionless competitive markets.

## 6 · Asset types
| Type | Simple meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Capital assets / cash market instruments** | Claims whose worth rests on expected cash flows | Shares, bonds, loans |
| **Derivatives** | Payoff linked to underlying asset or index; used for risk transfer or speculation | Futures, options |
| **Real assets** | Physical / productive assets | Property, oil, wheat |
| **Speculative / stores of value** | May not produce recurring cash flows | Gold, art |

> [!note] Caution
> These groups overlap in everyday language. The textbook separates gold/art as primarily stores of value, not as a formal mutually exclusive asset-class taxonomy.

## 7 · EMH, arbitrage & market failure
- **Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH):** prices rapidly reflect relevant information under the hypothesis; consistent **risk-adjusted** outperformance using already reflected information is difficult.
- **Arbitrage:** buy cheaper and sell dearer equivalent exposure; this **pushes** equivalent prices together, but costs, funding and constraints can prevent perfect equality.
- **Informational Efficiency ≠ Allocative Efficiency:** reacting to news quickly does not automatically mean capital funds the best real-world projects.
- **Bubble / Mispricing:** market prices may diverge from defensible fundamental valuations; 2007–08 crisis illustrates why this matters.
- **"No free lunch"** is a principle/ideal, **not** a guarantee that real-world prices are always correct.

## Mini exam drill — answer without looking
1. Why does a price rise when there is a shortage, and how might supply respond?
2. How does **Financial Investment** differ from **Real Investment**?
3. Why is £1,100 next year worth only ~£1,047.62 today at 5%?
4. What is the difference between **Risk Premium** and realised return?
5. What happens to a fixed-coupon bond when market yields rise — and why?
6. How do **P/E**, **P/B**, and **DCF** differ?
7. Can an informationally efficient market still allocate capital badly?

> [!tip] Exam sentence to memorise
> **Finance uses prices to allocate capital across people and time, while assessing, pricing and transferring risk. Its three pillars are time value of money, risk management and asset pricing.**
